# 10 — AI-Assisted Productivity and Governance

## 1. Appropriate AI use cases

- Summarize interview notes after removing unnecessary personal identifiers.
- Draft requirements, user stories and test cases from reviewed source notes.
- Compare alternative workflows and identify missing exception paths.
- Generate first-pass SQL explanations, data-quality checks and documentation.
- Create synthetic sample records and test scenarios.
- Assist with backlog refinement, glossary maintenance and release-note drafting.

## 2. Human accountability

AI-generated outputs are drafts, not approved business decisions. A named business analyst, product owner, architect, security reviewer or finance owner must validate outputs in their area of responsibility.

## 3. Data handling rules

- Do not upload client-confidential content, personal data, bank details, secrets, credentials or production exports to unapproved AI tools.
- Prefer synthetic or properly minimized datasets for demonstrations and prompts.
- Apply access controls, retention rules and approved vendor settings.
- Remove tokens, passwords, connection strings and private keys from source, logs and screenshots.
- Verify facts, formulas, citations, requirements and code before use.

## 4. Quality checklist

Before publishing AI-assisted work, verify:
1. Traceability to a source requirement or clearly labeled assumption.
2. Correct terminology and business rules.
3. No invented metrics or unsupported implementation claims.
4. Privacy, security and licensing concerns reviewed.
5. Acceptance criteria are testable and internally consistent.
6. The relevant owner has approved the final decision.

## 5. Demonstration evidence

For this portfolio simulation, synthetic data and explicitly hypothetical targets are used. No proprietary client data is required. The same governance approach should be adapted to the employer's policies, data classification and contractual obligations.
